## 1  Purpose

Give each platform domain ownership of its Terraform while keeping shared modules discoverable. This mirrors the model we already run in other clouds and lets security & IAM approve only what matters to them.

## 2  Guiding Considerations

### 2.1 Process

* Separation of Duties (SoD) across IAM, Policy, Pipelines, etc...
* Code cataloguing & re‑usability
* Avoid tight coupling / encourage functional layering

### 2.2 Operational

* Contain **blast radius** of global changes
* Consider approach/organization to *global* & *regional* resources
* Day‑0 support gaps (azapi > azurerm)
* Day‑1 support gaps (azapi > azurerm)
* Drift detection & reconciliation strategy

### 2.3 Security

* Secret management in TF state, code, etc. Cyberark source of truth
* Pre‑flight checks (Azure Policy, OPA/Sentinel)
* Least‑privilege pipelines

### 2.4 IAM

* Split principals for *plan* vs *apply*
* Dynamic credential rotation
* Identity resources isolated in **core‑iam** repo (see section 4)

### 2.5 SDLC / Pipelines

* Mandatory DSO approval for pipeline YAML
* Promotion gates & regression testing
* Static / vulnerability scanning

## 4  Spotlight: **core‑iam** Repo

 1. **Scope:** All identity constructs now scattered across the vendor repo.
 2. **Work:**
    * Strip identity blocks from existing code.
    * Reorganize into logical folders aligned with ownership and scope such as `team/`, `project/`, and `function/` 
    * Introduce PaC validation to ensure no identity creeps back in other repos.
3. **Outcome:** IAM team approves all IAM changes; other teams can submit PRs to identity resources, satisfying SoD.

## 5  Catalog Strategy

* **Goal:** Single home for *reusable* modules, versioned by SemVer.

* **Grouping:** Group and assign ownership based on modules purpose.

* **Future:** IAM will publish “blessed” permission‑set modules, reducing approval overhead.

## 6  Assumptions

* DSO must approve any edit to pipeline YAML files.
* Branch protection + enforce team reviews.

## 6.1 CODEOWNERS Considerations

CODEOWNERS could assist greatly in enforcing review boundaries in Git, but getting it right is **non‑trivial**. It requires careful engineering. A more detailed implementation guide will be provided separately; the specifics are **out of scope for this document**.

## 7  Alternatives & Trade-Offs & Trade‑Offs

If we could guarantee SoD via policy‑as‑code and Git controls alone, we could do significantly less refactoring and potentially even keep a mono‑repo for at least some time and rely on CODEOWNERS. This proposed split opts for clarity and lower cognitive load at the expense of extra repo management.
